# Data Quality and Exploratory Analysis

All numbers in this report come from `results/data_profile.json` and `results/data_quality.parquet`, generated by a complete streaming pass over the immutable raw artifacts. Source bytes and hashes are in `state/data_manifest.json`. Figures are generated from the same profile under `reports/figures/`.

## CFPB complaints

The official bulk ZIP contains **17,651,452 records**, with receipt dates from **2011-12-01 through 2026-09-09**. Complaint ID duplicate count was **0** in the complete scan. Historical narrative text is populated for **3,849,642 records (21.81%)** and missing for **13,801,810**. The hash-selected narrative-length sample contains 3,876 records; token-count quantiles were 32 (P10), 60 (P25), 115 (P50), 209 (P75), 360 (P90), and 925.5 (P99).

The file still contains a narrative column and historical values, but CFPB ceased discretionary publication of narratives on 2026-08-14. The structured corpus extends through 2026-09-09; this study does not interpret missing post-change narratives as ordinary missing-at-random observations. It uses historical public narrative text only with that cutoff caveat and uses deterministic synthetic language for enterprise benchmark truth.

The leading product labels demonstrate both extreme imbalance and taxonomy evolution:

The largest issue labels are “Incorrect information on your report” (7,954,474), “Improper use of your report” (3,376,102), and “Problem with a company's investigation into an existing problem” (2,290,380). These distributions justify macro-averaged classification metrics, temporal splits, taxonomy mapping, and drift evaluation rather than accuracy-led random-split reporting.

Complaint counts are **not a statistical sample and must not be interpreted as population prevalence**. Changes in intake, publication, taxonomy, and reporting behavior affect observed counts. The temporal-count figure is descriptive of records in this corpus only.

## NIST controls and assessments

The pinned OSCAL 5.1.1 catalog yielded **1,193 base controls plus enhancements** across 20 families and **3,418 relationship links**. The largest counts are SC (162), AC (147), SA (145), SI (118), IA (74), and AU (69). The official 800-53A CSV contains **5,383 assessment rows**. This creates an explicit many-object control/assessment grounding corpus rather than treating a control title as its complete test procedure.

Machine-readable OSCAL is used for deterministic IDs, families, statements, and relationships; normative publication text remains authoritative if a discrepancy appears. Counts reflect the pinned release and parser definition documented in `results/profiles/nist.json`.

## Title 12 CFR

The regulatory corpus contains three dated eCFR full-title snapshots (2024-01-01, 2025-01-01, 2026-01-01) plus all ten official 2024 GovInfo volumes. Together they occupy **174,542,485 bytes** and contain **43,768 section-like elements** under the documented XML tag heuristic.

The eCFR snapshots contain 7,176, 7,199, and 7,200 section-like elements respectively. That variation is direct evidence that “latest policy” is not a valid historical lookup rule, although element-count differences alone do not establish the legal significance of a change. Silver processing will retain document date, business-valid interval, system-known interval, source type, and hash for point-in-time retrieval.

## SEC filings

The bounded corpus contains **12 primary filing documents** for three financial-sector issuers: one recent 10-K and three recent 10-Qs per issuer, selected by filing date. Total raw size is **119,387,980 bytes**, producing approximately **2,496 non-overlap-equivalent 512-token chunks** under the rough word-count diagnostic.

Document size varies materially: most primary filings are 8.6–12.9 MB, while the selected WFC 2025 10-K primary HTML is about 0.36 MB. That outlier requires parser/chunk inspection; it is not silently dropped. SEC content supplies financial long-document context and distractors, not synthetic-case labels or internal-bank facts.

## Quality implications

- CFPB imbalance and taxonomy changes require mapped labels, temporal drift measures, macro metrics, and class-aware sampling.
- Narrative missingness is structural and publication-policy-dependent; text experiments must state their eligible population.
- NIST enhancements and assessment procedures require hierarchical/control-aware chunking and relationship-aware evaluation.
- CFR versions require business-valid and system-known time. Current-only retrieval is an explicit negative control.
- SEC HTML needs robust text extraction, broken-document quarantine, and per-document chunk diagnostics before indexing.
- Exact public-corpus facts remain separate from deterministic synthetic enterprise labels, preventing public-data frequency from becoming assumed enterprise ground truth.
